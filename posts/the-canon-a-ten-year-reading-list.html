The Canon—A Ten-Year Reading List

One of my New Year’s resolutions for this year has been to gain a deeper knowledge of the classics. Most of what I know about the great literary, scientific, and philosophical works of the Western tradition (and beyond) comes from indirect sources or patchy readings. Yet, I’ve noticed that the little contact I’ve had with these works has provided me with enormous pleasure and insight. And I want more of that. 

Another reason why I want to begin systematically reading the classics connects with a growing concern: I’ve become increasingly aware of the brevity of life. Time is limited, which means that the number of books that I’ll be able to read in my lifetime is limited too. This, which is already a problem in itself, is exacerbated by the fact that we live in a time when the number of books and articles available is just insane. We are flooded with information. 

This means that, unless you pick up carefully what you read, you can end up reading lots of meaningless stuff simply because profit-driven algorithms or greedy advertisers—who do not care about your development as a person, but rather as a consumer—have decided so. Due to this, it’s worth it to stop and think about how I want to spend the most valuable resource I have—time—when it comes to reading. In this sense, I see reading the classics as a vindication of my freedom and rationality to decide what is best for me, rather than leaving that decision to the engines of the market. 

For these reasons, I have set myself to read this fantastic list of works, drawn largely from the Western Canon, over the next ten years. Of course, I will probably make some changes, as the list is perhaps too Anglocentric, and I also want to include works from other traditions. I may also change the order of some readings if I feel so.

I cannot guarantee that I’ll be able to stick to the readings in the distant future, as I don’t know what it will bring. Still, I had made my promise that I’ll try to read all these books.

After each reading, I will also post brief summaries or personal reflections so that I can later revisit them and remember what the experience of reading the work was like. I will share them with you here, just in case that encourages you to start reading the classics.
